\paragraph{The judge is validated by the human data.} Across the 40 system--query cells covered by
both, human graded relevance and the judge's overall-quality score agree strongly (Spearman
$\rho{=}0.75$, $p{=}2\times10^{-8}$) and rank the annotated systems identically. An annotator
explicitly instructed that lexical overlap is not evidence of relevance reaches the same ordering as
the judge, so the rubric-bias worry is not realised.

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:trackb} gives Track B scores for all ten systems on both
corpora, complete-case over all 200 queries with no judge failures. The picture is the reverse of
Track A's. \textbf{BM25 is last of ten on both corpora} ($1.90$ on \textit{Fann or Flop}, $1.70$ on
\textit{Diwan}), and is beaten significantly by eight of the nine dense encoders on each (paired
Wilcoxon, Holm-corrected; $\Delta$ up to $-1.04$ against Qwen3-8B, $p{=}2\times10^{-18}$). The
exception on both corpora is LaBSE, the weakest dense encoder, which BM25 is statistically
indistinguishable from ($p{=}.35$ on \textit{Diwan}, $p{=}.054$ on \textit{Fann or Flop}). The blind human judgments give
the same ordering on the systems they cover: on \textit{Fann or Flop}, jina-v3 $0.93$, the RRF
hybrid $0.83$, BM25 $0.50$ (paired Wilcoxon BM25 vs jina-v3 $p{=}.016$; pooled over both corpora
$\Delta{=}-0.45$, $p{=}.005$).

\paragraph{A second, open-weights judge replicates it.} A proprietary judge cannot be re-run once
withdrawn, so we repeat Track B with Gemma-3-27B-it locally, on the identical rubric, over a random
half of the thematic queries ($2{,}000$ cells, no parse failures). The judges agree at the cell
level (Spearman $\rho{=}0.67$ on overall quality) and on system ordering (Kendall
$\tau{=}{+}0.85$ on \textit{Diwan}, ${+}0.81$ on \textit{Fann or Flop}), and the second judge tracks
the blind human judgments too ($\rho{=}0.70$). One qualification: BM25 is \emph{ninth} of ten under it rather than last, still significantly below eight of nine dense encoders on both corpora, displaced by LaBSE, the one system the primary judge could not separate from BM25 either (Appendix~\ref{app:judge2}).

\paragraph{The judge separates matching from aboutness.} BM25 is last of ten on every aspect
requiring knowledge of what a poem is about, with its largest deficits on metaphor understanding and
semantic coverage; but on \emph{direct textual relevance}, which measures how far retrieved poems
merely repeat the query's wording, it is \emph{first} of ten on both corpora ($4.26$, $4.48$;
second of ten under the second judge, behind LaBSE; Appendix~\ref{app:aspects}). The judge
independently identifies BM25 as the most surface-matching system while ranking it last overall. This is convergent evidence for \S\ref{sec:bridge} from a channel sharing nothing with our lexical statistics, and rules out the reading that the judge merely penalises lexical retrieval.
